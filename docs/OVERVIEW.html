What was actually broken

v1 → v2

Four things in the old code could not be fixed in place — each one is a structural decision, not a bug you patch.

Revenue was counted twice on every cash sale

Before

Each sale posted a sale row and a receipt row between the same two accounts. Credit sales were posted against a customer row id instead of the customer's account, so no statement ever showed them.

After

One balanced entry per sale, written by the only class allowed to write entries. Debits must equal credits or the write is refused — and every customer has a real sub-account under receivables.

Stock lived in three places at once

Before

item_count, item_base_quantity and current_stock all held a quantity. Editing an invoice deleted its transactions but left the stock movements behind, so the numbers drifted apart quietly.

After

One append-only stock ledger. The quantity on the item card is a cache maintained by database triggers — application code cannot write it even by accident.

A purchase never changed what an item cost

Before

The line total was multiplied by the quantity a second time, the supplier discount and the freight went nowhere, and the item's cost price was left at whatever had been typed months earlier. Every profit figure inherited that.

After

Discount and freight are spread across the lines by value, to the last unit, giving a landed cost per base unit that becomes the item's moving average.

Reports were pinned to account id numbers

Before

Views read account_id IN (7, 8, 9, 10) and the cashier controller hardcoded 7, 10, 34, 44, 87. Renaming an account, or importing the chart onto a new machine, broke the books silently.

After

Accounts carry a stable system_key — cash, sales, cogs. Nothing in the app writes an account id as a literal.

How a sale is priced

one calculator, six steps

The screen, the receipt, the stock movement and the ledger all read the same result object. There is no second place where a total is worked out, which is what used to let the cart and the printed invoice disagree.

  1. Line gross — unit price × quantity, in the unit being sold.

  2. Line discount — a percentage of that line or a typed amount, never more than the line. A gift is simply a 100 % discount.

  3. Bill discount — worked out on the discounted lines, then allocated back onto them by largest remainder, exactly. Each line knows what it really sold for, so per-item profit survives a round number off the total.

  4. Tax — per line, on the line's final net; prices may include it or exclude it.

  5. Service charge, then cash rounding to the configured step, with the difference kept as its own figure and posted to its own account.

  6. Tenders — change comes only out of cash; overpaying by card leaves a credit, not a handful of notes.

Worked example Qty Price Gross Share of bill discount Line net
Cola, sold by the piece 3 1 250 3 750 −375 3 375
Chips 2 750 1 500 −150 1 350
Subtotal after a 10 % bill discount 5 250 −525 4 725
Rounded to the nearest 250 (+25 to the rounding account) 4 750

Figures in IQD. The 525 discount splits 375 / 150 — not 262.5 each — because it follows the value of the lines.

Buying in cartons, selling in pieces

landed cost

An item is not a thing with a price — it is a thing with a price per unit. Each unit is a row with its own barcode and its own factor back to the base unit, so the carton barcode adds a carton and the shelf still counts pieces. 

10 cartons  × 12 pieces  @ 12 000 per carton   = 120 000
freight                                        +   2 400
                                               ──────────
landed                                           122 400  → 120 pieces
                                                            @ 1 020 each

That 1 020 is what the item's moving average becomes, and it is the number every future profit figure is measured against. In the old code this line would have booked 122 400 × 120.

The database

schema v1

Books

  • accounts — chart, with system_key
  • journal_entries / journal_lines
  • parties — customers, suppliers, staff
  • payments / payment_allocations

Goods

  • items · item_units · categories · units
  • stock_moves — the ledger, with triggers

Documents

  • sales_invoices + lines + sale_tenders
  • purchase_invoices + lines
  • carts — held sales, survive a power cut

Reporting views

  • v_trial_balance · v_general_ledger
  • v_party_balances · v_daily_sales
  • v_unbalanced_entries — always empty

Foreign keys enforced, WAL journaling, every query parameterised. Full column reference in docs/DATABASE.md.

Bringing the old data across

settings → import

Categories, units, items with all their units and barcodes, customers and suppliers, the whole Arabic chart of accounts, opening stock at last known cost, and each account's closing balance as one opening entry. Old sales invoices come over as read-only history.

The old journal is deliberately not replayed. Its cash sales were double counted and its credit purchases were reversed; replaying it would import the errors along with the data. Balances are taken at face value — check them once against your own records before the first sale.
